A cleared lot is not automatically a build ready lot

Poor clearing leaves buried root systems, unstable organic material, hidden debris and uneven subsoil, and those create settlement and drainage problems months or years after construction finishes. In wooded parts of Saratoga County, careless clearing also raises the risk of erosion, standing water and regrowth that interferes with future site access or utility work.

Professional clearing is about controlling what stays beneath the surface as much as what comes off the top. Stumps, root networks, buried demolition debris and uncompacted disturbed soil all change how the land performs once structures, pavement, utilities or drainage go in. The goal is a stable site that supports every phase that follows.

Signs a property needs clearing or corrective prep

  • Dense vegetation is limiting property access or visibility
  • Old stumps or root systems are surfacing through the soil
  • Sections of the lot stay muddy long after rainfall
  • Fallen trees or unmanaged growth are obstructing future work areas
  • Previous clearing left buried debris, concrete or unusable fill
  • Construction equipment cannot safely access the property as it stands

Every job starts with a site walk

We assess what is coming out, what is staying, and what the land needs to look like when we are done, before any equipment comes onto the property.

Permits and wetlands

Tree removal in New York may require permits depending on the municipality and the size or species involved, and the City of Saratoga Springs has specific regulations for certain properties. For sites in or near wetlands or stream corridors, DEC permits may apply. We identify regulatory requirements before clearing starts, not after.

Ground conditions matter

Heavy equipment on soft spring soil causes rutting and compaction that complicates grading later. Late spring through fall is the window for full clearing.

Site clearing questions

Do I need a permit to clear trees or brush in Saratoga Springs?

It depends on the property, the location and the species. The City of Saratoga Springs regulates tree removal in certain areas, and properties near wetlands or waterways may require DEC permits. We review the site and advise on permit requirements before any clearing begins. Do not assume it is permit free.

What is the best time of year to clear a lot?

Late spring through fall is the practical window. Frozen or saturated ground in early spring makes it hard to move equipment without rutting and compacting the soil, which complicates grading later. Winter clearing is possible but adds complexity. Storm damage cleanup happens year round regardless of season.

Do you handle debris removal and haul off?

Yes. Debris removal is included in the clearing scope. We do not leave a cleared lot full of brush piles and stumps. Everything that comes off the site gets hauled away as part of the job.

Can you clear a lot that has a structure on it?

Yes. We handle structure demolition and removal as part of site clearing. Scope depends on structure type, size, and any materials needing special handling such as asbestos or lead paint. We assess the structure during the estimate and factor demolition into the plan.

How long does lot clearing take?

A standard residential lot with brush, shrubs, a few trees and debris removal typically takes one to three days depending on density and access. Larger commercial parcels or properties with structures take longer.

Do you coordinate with excavation and grading crews?

We are the excavation and grading crew. Clearing, excavation and grading are all ours, so we sequence our own work without gaps between phases and can take a site from raw lot to excavated and graded under one contract.
