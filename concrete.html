Concrete is durable when it is installed correctly

That means a properly compacted gravel base, the right mix design for the application, control joints placed to manage where cracking happens, and enough cure time before load goes on it.

In the Capital Region it also means accounting for frost depth. Slabs and footings that ignore frost depth, or that sit on a poorly drained sub base, will move. Segmental block, natural stone and poured concrete all perform well against Saratoga's freeze thaw cycle when they are set on a compacted gravel base below the frost line. Reinforced and properly drained poured concrete is the most freeze resistant of the three.

Signs the clock is running

Cracks wider than a quarter inch, especially diagonal or horizontal breaks through the slab. Surface scaling or flaking. Sections heaved more than half an inch above the adjacent concrete. A garage slab cracked through or actively settling. Any shift large enough to create a trip hazard on a walkway or entry.

Patios and outdoor slabs

A concrete patio is a durable, low maintenance alternative to pavers or wood decking. We pour standard broom finished slabs as well as stamped and decorative concrete. Repair, resurface or replace

Repair covers surface cracks, minor spalling and isolated pop-outs. Crack filling and sealing extend a slab's life meaningfully when the base is sound.

Resurfacing handles widespread surface deterioration without structural failure. A bonded overlay restores appearance and function without full demolition.

Replacement is the answer for heaved or sunken slabs, a compromised sub base, or rebar corrosion. Patching will not hold and full replacement is the correct long term decision.

Concrete questions we get

How do freeze thaw cycles damage concrete here?

Water expands about 9 percent when it freezes. When it gets into cracks or surface pores and then freezes overnight, it forces those openings wider. Over dozens of cycles in a Capital Region winter, a surface that looked intact in October can be actively spalling by April.

Should I repair or replace my concrete driveway?

It comes down to the sub base. If the compacted base under the slab is sound and the damage is limited to the surface or isolated cracks, repair or resurfacing is usually right. If the slab has heaved, settled significantly, cracked through in several places, or sections sound hollow when tapped, the base has shifted or eroded and replacement costs less over time than patching a failing foundation.

What is the difference between spalling and cracking?

Cracking is a structural break through the slab. Spalling is surface delamination, meaning the top layer flaking or peeling away, usually from freeze thaw cycles combined with de-icing salt penetration. Spalling does not always mean the slab has failed structurally, but once it is widespread and deep, resurfacing stops being a lasting fix.

Can concrete be poured in winter in the Capital Region?

Cold weather work is possible with pre-heated materials, accelerating admixtures where appropriate, and insulated blankets during cure. We do not pour when temperatures are forecast below 20 degrees Fahrenheit in the first 24 hours. For most residential projects we recommend scheduling between May and October.

Does concrete outlast asphalt?

Under heavy load, yes, and it takes less ongoing maintenance because there is no sealing cycle to keep up with. Asphalt is cheaper to install and more forgiving of minor base movement. Which one is right depends on load, budget and how the surface will be used.
