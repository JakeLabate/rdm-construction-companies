Signs the base has gone

Some driveways can be saved with sealcoating or crack repair. Others have failed below the surface, where no maintenance product reaches. These are the tells.

  • Alligator cracking, the interconnected web pattern that signals base failure
  • Potholes that come back after every repair
  • Sections that are sinking, heaving or separating
  • Standing water that pools hours after rain
  • Crumbling edges along the sides of the driveway
  • A surface past 20 years old that needs repairs every season

New installation, overlay or repair

New installation or full replacement is right when the base has failed, or when you are paving a gravel or dirt driveway for the first time. It costs more up front and it is the only fix that lasts when the foundation is the problem.

Asphalt overlay adds a new 1.5 to 2 inch layer over structurally sound existing pavement, at roughly half the cost of full replacement. It works when the damage is cosmetic rather than structural.

Repair and maintenance handles isolated potholes, cracks and oxidation. If that is all your driveway needs, we will say so.

What it costs in Saratoga County

Most full asphalt driveway installations run between $4 and $7 per square foot including removal of the old surface. A typical two car driveway of 600 to 700 square feet usually lands between $2,800 and $5,000. Overlays run $2 to $4 per square foot when the existing base qualifies.

Rural properties with 200 foot runs, or jobs needing significant drainage work or grading, run proportionally higher. Every estimate is itemized so you can see what is driving the number.

How we build a driveway

Four stages, in this order, every time. Stage three is where driveways are won or lost in this climate.

  1. Evaluation. We walk the property, check the existing surface and drainage, measure the area, and deliver a free itemized written estimate. No vague allowances and no surprise add-ons.
  2. Excavation and removal. We remove the old surface and excavate to proper depth. Because excavation is our own trade, grading and drainage problems get corrected at this stage rather than paved over.
  3. Grading and base preparation. We grade for water runoff and build a compacted crushed gravel base, typically 6 to 8 inches, so the asphalt above it stays stable through every freeze and thaw.
  4. Asphalt installation and compaction. Hot mix laid at 2.5 to 3 inches compacted thickness and rolled to a smooth, uniform finish with clean edges and transitions.

Driveway paving questions

Should I repave my driveway or resurface it with an overlay?

It depends on the base. If the driveway has isolated cracking but no sinking and no alligator cracking, an overlay can restore it at roughly half the cost. If the base has failed, an overlay will mirror the old cracks within a year or two. We assess both surface and base on every estimate before recommending an approach.

How long does a new asphalt driveway last here?

Fifteen to twenty five years in the Capital Region. The three factors that move that number are base preparation at install, sealcoating every two to three years, and prompt crack repair before water gets below the surface.

How long before I can drive on it?

You can walk on it after about 24 hours and drive on it after two to three days. Avoid parking in the same spot for the first week or two in hot weather, and avoid turning the wheel while stopped. Asphalt keeps curing for several months after installation.

When should a new driveway be sealcoated for the first time?

Wait six to twelve months so the asphalt fully cures, then sealcoat every two to three years. Sealing too early traps oils in fresh asphalt, and sealing every year causes buildup and peeling.

How thick should an asphalt driveway be?

For residential driveways in Saratoga County we install 2.5 to 3 inches of compacted asphalt over a 6 to 8 inch compacted gravel base. Driveways that carry heavier vehicles such as RVs or trailers need a thicker section, which we spec during the estimate.

Can a gravel driveway be paved over directly?

Not directly. Loose gravel that was never installed as a compacted base layer will not support asphalt. The process is to grade the existing gravel, compact it as a starting point, build a proper stone base on top of that, and install asphalt from there.
