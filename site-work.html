The visible structure is only as good as what is under it

Improper grading, unstable soil, poor drainage planning or a badly installed utility trench create water and structural problems that get more expensive to correct after the build is finished. Site preparation is where stable support, controlled drainage and accurate elevations get established, before concrete, pavement, utilities or foundations exist.

Reasons property owners call for site work

  • Water collects near foundations or in low areas after storms
  • Existing grading sends runoff toward structures or pavement
  • Utility lines need replacement, relocation or expansion
  • A site needs elevation changes before construction can begin
  • Drainage problems are causing erosion or soil displacement
  • Construction access or groundwork has to be established first

Soil across Saratoga County

Soils here range from sandy glacial deposits near Wilton to heavier clay in Malta and Ballston Spa. The two behave differently for drainage and load bearing, and that difference changes how a dig is planned and executed.

Spring is the hard season

Saratoga Springs takes roughly 45 inches of precipitation a year, with the most erosive stretch concentrated in March and April as snowmelt runs across exposed or badly graded sites. Positive drainage established at the start is what keeps that melt off your foundation.

Trenching

Trenching cuts the narrow, precise channels underground utilities need. Unlike open excavation it demands accuracy in depth, slope and width to meet code and to make the utility perform as designed over decades. New York requires utilities buried at code specified depths against freeze damage, and water lines in Saratoga County typically go in at 48 inches or deeper.

  • Water and sewer line trenches
  • Electrical and telecommunications conduit runs
  • Drainage pipe installation
  • Utility relocation
  • Foundation drain and perimeter drainage systems

Site grading

Grading shapes the land before and after construction. Done right it moves water away from foundations, creates a stable base for pavement and structures, and controls erosion on disturbed sites. Done wrong it is among the most common causes of chronic basement moisture, driveway heave and landscape washout, and each of those gets worse every spring.

  • Rough grading for new construction sites
  • Final grading and finish work
  • Driveway excavation and grading
  • Drainage swale creation
  • Yard and lawn grading for drainage correction
  • Base grading for parking lots, driveways and access roads

Lot and site clearing

Professional clearing for residential, commercial and new construction projects of any size. Trees, brush, overgrowth and debris removed, then land prepared for foundations, driveways, utilities and development. Retaining wall design and installation

We design and build retaining walls that do more than hold back soil. A wall done properly creates usable outdoor space, improves drainage, and adds long term structure and curb appeal. The base goes in compacted and below the frost line, which is the difference between a wall that stands and a wall that leans.

Which service applies

  1. Excavation when you are breaking ground: a foundation, basement, septic system or major site prep for a new structure.
  2. Trenching when you are installing or replacing underground lines: water, sewer, electrical, communications or perimeter drainage.
  3. Grading when the surface itself has to be reshaped, for drainage correction, driveway base prep or final finish after construction.

Many projects need all three in that order. Running the sequence under one contract removes the coordination gaps that come with a separate subcontractor for each phase.

Site work questions we get

How deep does excavation need to go in Saratoga Springs?

Frost depth in Saratoga County runs roughly 48 to 54 inches. Any footing or foundation element has to sit at or below the frost line or it will heave. We excavate to the depth your engineer specifies, always at or below frost depth for structural work, and we do not leave open excavations exposed through a freeze period.

Do I need permits for excavation here?

In most cases yes. Foundation excavation and utility work generally require permits from the City of Saratoga Springs or Saratoga County depending on the project type and location. We work with property owners to identify permit requirements before work starts.

What is the 811 DIG SAFE requirement?

Before any excavation or trenching in New York, contractors are required by law to call 811. That triggers a utility locate service which marks underground gas, electric, water and communications lines on the property. We call 811 before every trench and every dig, without exception.

What does OSHA require for trench safety?

Any trench five feet deep or more requires a protective system, meaning sloped walls, shoring or a trench box. All RDM trenching on commercial jobs complies with OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P. Workers do not enter unprotected trenches at that depth.

What is the difference between rough grading and finish grading?

Rough grading moves the bulk of the earth to establish approximate elevations and slopes. Finish grading is precision work that creates the final surface contours for drainage, planting and pavement base. New construction typically needs both.

How long does site grading take?

A typical residential grading job, drainage correction or final grade for a driveway or new lawn, runs one to three days depending on site size and conditions. Large commercial site preparation can run several weeks. We give a realistic timeline at the estimate.
